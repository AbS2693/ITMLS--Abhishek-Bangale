
EXERCISE 6.5 - TASK 3: GRAD-CAM VERIFICATION REPORT
====================================================

OBJECTIVE:
==========
Verify that Grad-CAM heatmaps correctly highlight the target objects in images
(pedestrians, traffic lights, vehicles).

METHODOLOGY:
============

For each model and visualization, we directly compare the Grad-CAM heatmap's
highest-activation (red) region against the actual location of the target
object in the original image, rather than describing the heatmap in isolation.

ANALYSIS BY MODEL:
==================

PEDESTRIAN MODEL
----------------
Images analyzed: pedestrian_gradcam_1, pedestrian_gradcam_2, pedestrian_gradcam_3

FINDING - Image 1 (pedestrian_gradcam_1):
  A pedestrian is present far down the road, roughly center-frame, small.
  The hottest activation is a solid red band across the TOP of the image
  (sky / tree-line), not on the pedestrian, which falls in a low-activation
  (blue) region.
  VERDICT: FAILED - the model is not attending to the pedestrian in this image.

FINDING - Image 2 (pedestrian_gradcam_2):
  Two pedestrians are clearly visible on the left side of the frame.
  The hottest, most concentrated red activation sits almost exactly on
  these two pedestrians.
  VERDICT: PASSED - correct localization.

FINDING - Image 3 (pedestrian_gradcam_3):
  A pedestrian (in yellow) is visible center-left of the frame. There is
  moderate (yellow/green) activation near this region, but the single
  hottest red spot is on the right edge of the frame (building/parked car
  area), away from the pedestrian.
  VERDICT: PARTIAL - some relevant activation near the pedestrian, but the
  strongest response is on an unrelated region.

Pedestrian model summary: 1 of 3 sampled images show clean, correct
localization on the pedestrian; 1 shows a clear spurious-feature failure
(sky); 1 is mixed. This is NOT a uniform "PASSED" result.

TRAFFIC LIGHT MODEL

Images analyzed: traffic_light_gradcam_1, traffic_light_gradcam_2, traffic_light_gradcam_3

FINDING - Images 1, 2, 3:
  None of the three sampled images contain a visible traffic light at all
  (they are the same underlying street scenes used for the pedestrian

  VERDICT: NOT VERIFIABLE from these samples - the sampled images do not

TRAFFIC LIGHT MODEL SUMMARY: This sample selection does not support a
"PASSED" verdict, because none of the three chosen images have a traffic
light in them. A re-sample using images that genuinely contain a visible
traffic light is needed before any localization claim can be made.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT:
===================

The heatmaps are structured (not random noise) and vary across images, which
is consistent with the models having learned real, non-trivial features.
However, checking the heatmaps directly against object locations shows the
models - particularly the pedestrian and vehicle detectors - frequently
attend to sky, tree-line, or background regions rather than the target
object. This is consistent with, and helps explain, the poor pedestrian
recall (0.483 on the in-distribution test set) already established in
Exercise 3/4: the model's attention is not reliably anchored to the object
it is supposed to be detecting.

The traffic-light sample cannot be assessed at all with the current image
selection, since none of the three sampled images contain a traffic light.

SAFETY IMPLICATIONS:
====================
  - The pedestrian and vehicle models show real evidence of spurious
    background/sky reliance in at least one of three sampled images each,
    not just a hypothetical risk (compare Exercise 6.6 Task 1, which
    reasons about exactly this failure mode).
  - This is a genuine, image-verified finding, not an assumption: the
    heatmap for pedestrian_gradcam_1 and vehicle_gradcam_1 both show a
    prominent sky/top-of-frame activation band unrelated to any target
    object.
  - A "reliable" localization claim cannot be made from this sample; more
    images per model, deliberately including confirmed target-object
    presence, are needed for a defensible verification pass.

RECOMMENDATIONS:
================

1. Re-select image samples for the traffic-light model so that at least
   three actually contain a visible traffic light before drawing any
   localization conclusion for that model.
2. Investigate the sky/top-of-frame activation pattern seen for the
   pedestrian and vehicle models - this may explain part of the recall gap
   documented in Exercise 3/4 and should be treated as a residual risk, not
   a passed check.
3. Increase the sample size beyond three images per model before treating
   any localization verdict as representative.
4. Next Steps: Task 4 (failure-case analysis), Exercise 6.6 (distribution
   shift evaluation).
